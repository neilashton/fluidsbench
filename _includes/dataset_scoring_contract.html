Final predictions must cover the complete reference support. For every scored case, this means every required entity, exactly once, in the actual field-bearing mesh or curve file from the named public dataset release. FluidsBench binds those files, their ordering, any masks, and their hashes to the scoring-support release. A method may infer on another representation and may process a large file in chunks, but it must map its predictions back to every required reference entity before calculating the final metrics. Downsampled or partially covered predictions are not complete results.

How to calculate large cases

  • Surface and one-dimensional curve fields use the physical measure (area or length) as the primary relative L2 and an equal-entity relative L2 as the secondary value. Two- and three-dimensional flow domains reverse that order: equal-entity is primary and physical cell-area or cell-volume weighting is secondary.
  • Face- or cell-associated fields use the face area, cell area, or cell volume directly. For PointData or CGNS Vertex fields, the physical weight is a deterministic mass-lumped dual area, dual volume, or dual length derived from the same reference mesh connectivity.
  • In one pass over each case, accumulate the squared-error numerator and ground-truth denominator for both the equal-entity and physical-weight variants, together with entity count and total physical weight. Chunks may be any convenient size, but their numerators and denominators must be summed before taking the square root; never average chunk-level L2 values.
  • Calculate each complete-case percentage first, then macro-average the case percentages so every case counts equally.

Sharing complete prediction fields is optional. A contributor may link versioned predictions in an external public repository, but the submission package itself stays small and records exact coverage plus the per-case sufficient statistics used to reproduce both relative-L2 values.
